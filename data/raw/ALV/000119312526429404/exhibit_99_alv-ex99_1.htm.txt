Key business developments in the third quarter of 2026
•
Net sales decreased organically* by 1.1%
,

which was 0.8pp higher than the global LVP decrease of 1.9% (Mobility Global, Oct 2026) mainly driven by strong performance in Asia. Regional and customer LVP mix is estimated to have impacted sales negatively by around 0.9pp.

Comments from Mikael Bratt, President & CEO

 

 

We continued to deliver well across all key areas in the third quarter. Our sales in India grew by nearly 30% and our sales to Chinese OEMs grew by more than 20%. Strong sales growth in Japan contributed to our sales in Asia exceeding China in the quarter. Globally, our organic sales performance exceeded global LVP by around 1pp, significantly outpacing LVP growth in India, Japan and with Chinese OEMs.

To support our growing partnerships with Chinese OEMs, our innovation capabilities, global competitiveness and long term growth, we opened our second R&D center in China in Q3.

The business environment remains uncertain but we anticipate a strong fourth quarter supported by an increasing number of product launches and significant raw material recoveries.

Throughout the year, cost headwinds have increased materially, mainly related to raw materials, logistics, the recent flooding in Thailand and lower call off accuracy since Q1. We have been able to offset the majority of these cost headwinds mainly through labor efficiency, material savings through Value Engineering and successful negotiations with customers and suppliers. However, the additional headwinds narrows our full year adjusted operating margin expectation to around 10.5%, the low end of our range of around 10.5-11%. We continue to expect unchanged organic sales growth and an operating cash flow of around $1.2 billion.
Based on our full year guidance, we continue to expect strong cash flow for the year, which supports our ambition to provide attractive shareholder returns through share repurchases and dividends.
Finally, I would like to invite you to our 2027 Investor Day, which will take place in Shanghai, China, on April 26 and 27, 2027. It will be held in connection with the Auto Shanghai, the largest and most important auto show in the world.

We continued to successfully manage geopolitical developments during the quarter, limiting the effects of tariffs, supply chain challenges and raw material price increases. Well-executed cost reduction initiatives and increased engineering income contributed to a third quarter adjusted operating margin of 9.7%.
Our cash flow remained strong, supporting our ambitious shareholder return strategy. Our leverage ratio of 1.3x was almost unchanged compared with the prior quarter, despite repurchasing around 1.23 million shares for $150 million and paying dividends of $63 million in the quarter. Over the last 12 months, we have repurchased 4.14 million shares for $500 million, in addition to paying $258 million in dividends and keeping our debt leverage ratio unchanged.

Business and market condition update
Supply Chain
Customer call-off accuracy deteriorated slightly compared to Q3 2025 as well as compared to Q2 2026, driven by development in China. We expect call-off volatility for the full year 2026 on average to be slightly improved compared to 2025. However, the continued significant uncertainty in the geopolitical environment and future changes in tariffs and trade restrictions may lead to more negative call-off volatility.
Raw material inflation, geopolitical risks and tariffs
Raw material price changes had a negative impact on our profitability in the third quarter, with a gross impact of around $42 million. For the full year 2026, we currently estimate a gross impact of around $120 million from higher raw material prices. We expect to be able to continue to mitigate a majority of this headwind, mainly through internal cost reductions, material mix improvements and commercial negotiations with customers and suppliers. Given the continued uncertainty in the geopolitical environment, tariffs and trade restrictions may contribute to a more adverse inflationary environment. We continue to implement productivity and cost-reduction initiatives to offset these cost pressures.
The tariffs imposed in 2025 negatively impacted our profitability in the third quarter of 2026. We achieved customer compensation for more than 80% of the tariff costs, resulting in a net negative impact after compensation of around $6 million, in line with the net impact in the third quarter of 2025. Including the dilution effect, the impact on operating margin was around 35 bps negative. While it is our ambition and expectation to continue passing tariff costs on to our customers, there is significant uncertainty as future recovery levels may vary. For the full year 2026, we estimate the tariff-related dilution on operating margin will be similar to the around 20 bps experienced for full year 2025.
Ongoing geopolitical developments, including hostilities in and around the Persian Gulf, have increased uncertainty in the global economic environment. These conditions may affect supply chains, commodity prices, customer demand, and broader market stability. As a result, our current financial guidance reflects the best information available today but may change if these geopolitical dynamics materially affect our operations or the markets in which we operate.

We continue to closely monitor both geopolitical developments and the tariff policy environment in order to remain agile and to adjust our commercial and operational responses to any such developments.

Sales for Seatbelt Products and Other declined organically* by 3.9% in the quarter. Sales decreased organically in China, Americas and EMEA while they increased in Asia excluding China.
 

 

 
Sales by region
Our global organic sales* decreased by 1.1% compared to the global LVP decrease of 1.9% (according to Mobility Global, Oct 2026). The relative outperformance was positively impacted by product launches but negatively impacted by regional and model LVP mix development (around 90 bps). Third quarter 2026 development
Gross profit
 decreased by $26 million, and gross margin decreased by 0.9pp compared to the prior year. The decrease in gross profit was mainly attributable to the $13 million gross profit impact of the supplier settlement compensation reported in Q3 2025, lower net sales and higher logistics costs.
S,G&A
 costs increased by $1 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to slightly higher IT expenses and professional services costs partly offset by slightly lower personnel costs. S,G&A costs in relation to sales were unchanged at 5.1%.
R,D&E, net,
 costs decreased by $18 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to $26 million of higher engineering income related to timing effects, partly offset by adverse effects from FX translation, IT expenses and personnel costs. R,D&E, net, in relation to sales decreased from 4.3% to 3.7%.
Other income (expense), net,
was negative $5 million, compared to negative $1 million in the same period last year. The negative $5 million in Q3 2026 consisted mainly of capacity alignment costs related to our restructuring activities in Türkiye.

 

 
Operating income
 decreased by $13 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to lower gross profit and higher capacity alignment costs, partly offset by lower R,D&E, net, costs, as outlined above.
Adjusted operating income*
decreased by $9 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to the lower gross profit, partly offset by lower R,D&E, net, costs, as outlined above.
Financial and non-operating items, net,
 was a negative $24 million compared to a negative $27 million a year earlier. The improvement was mainly due to lower costs for non-operating items mainly related to positive FX effects.
Income before taxes
 decreased by $10 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to lower operating income, partly offset by lower costs for financial and non-operating items, net, as outlined above.

Tax rate
 was 32.5% compared to 26.9% in the prior year. Discrete tax items, net, had an unfavorable impact of 4.9pp in Q3 2026, while discrete tax items, net, had a favorable impact of 1.3pp in Q3 2025. Discrete tax items recorded in Q3 2026 primarily related to negative tax impacts from the closure of tax audits during the quarter.
Earnings per share, diluted
 decreased by $0.15 compared to the prior year. The main drivers were negative impacts of $0.16 from higher taxes and $0.13 from lower operating income partly offset by $0.11 from a lower number of outstanding shares, diluted and $0.05 from lower financial and non-operating items.

First nine months 2026 development
Gross profit
 increased by $30 million while gross margin decreased by 0.2pp compared to the prior year. The drivers behind the gross profit improvement were mainly positive effects of higher sales and positive FX translation effects. This was partly offset by $25 million impact from a supplier compensation booked in Q3 2025 which was reversed in Q2 2026, and asset impairment related to the restructuring activities in Türkiye.
S,G&A
 costs increased by $10 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to negative FX translation effects and higher personnel costs, partly offset by the revision of an estimated credit loss reserve. S,G&A costs in relation to sales were unchanged at 5.3%.
R,D&E, net,
 costs increased by $22 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to higher personnel costs and negative FX translation effects, partly offset by higher engineering income. R,D&E, net, in relation to sales increased from 4.0% to 4.1%.
Other income (expense), net,
was negative $70 million, compared to positive $13 million in the same period last year. The increase in costs was mainly due to higher capacity alignment costs related to restructuring activities in Türkiye.
 

 

 
Operating income
 decreased by $86 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to higher capacity alignment costs related to restructuring activities in Türkiye, higher R,D&E, net, costs and higher S,G&A costs, partly offset by higher gross profit as outlined above.
Adjusted operating income*
decreased by $1 million compared to the prior year, due to the higher costs for R,D&E, net and S,G&A offset by the higher gross profit, as outlined above.
Financial and non-operating items, net,
 was negative $97 million compared to negative $76 million a year earlier. The increase in costs was mainly due to higher non-operating items, mainly related to costs associated with restructuring activities in Türkiye and Mexico.
Income before taxes
 decreased by $107 million compared to the prior year, mainly due to lower operating income and higher costs for financial and non-operating items, net, as outlined above.

Tax rate
 was 32.2% compared to 26.4% in the prior year. Earnings per share, diluted
 decreased by $1.23 compared to the prior year. The main drivers were negative impacts of $0.82 from lower operating income, $0.43 from higher tax and $0.18 from financial and non-operating items, partly offset by $0.22 from a lower number of outstanding shares, diluted.

Third quarter 2026 development
Changes in operating working capital
had a $45 million negative impact on operating cash flow compared to a negative impact of $53 million in the prior year. The $45 million increase in operating working capital resulted mainly from $71 million in higher inventories and $21 million from receivables, net, partly offset by $52 million from accounts payable and accrued liabilities.
Operating cash flow
 decreased by $19 million to $239 million compared to the prior year, mainly because of lower net income, partly offset by a smaller increase in operating working capital compared to last year.

 

 
Capital expenditure, net,
increased by $9 million compared to the prior year. The level of capital expenditure, net, in relation to sales increased to 4.3% versus 3.9% a year earlier.
Free operating cash flow*
 was positive $124 million compared to positive $153 million in the prior year. The decrease was due to lower operating cash flow and higher capital expenditure, net, as outlined above.
Cash conversion*
 defined as free operating cash flow* in relation to net income, was 80% compared to 87% a year earlier.

RECONCILIATION OF GAAP TO NON-GAAP MEASURES
In this report we sometimes refer to Non-GAAP measures that we and securities analysts use in measuring Autoliv's performance. We believe that these measures assist investors and management in analyzing trends in the Company's business for the reasons given below. Investors should not consider these Non-GAAP measures as substitutes, but rather as additions, to financial reporting measures prepared in accordance with GAAP. It should be noted that these measures, as defined, may not be comparable to similarly titled measures used by other companies.
Components in Sales Increase/Decrease
Since the Company historically generates approximately 75% of sales in currencies other than in the reporting currency (i.e., U.S. dollars) and currency rates have been volatile, we analyze the Company's sales trends and performance as changes in organic sales growth. This presents the increase or decrease in the overall U.S. dollar net sales on a comparable basis, allowing separate discussions of the impact of acquisitions/divestitures and exchange rates. The tables on pages 5 and 6 present changes in organic sales growth as reconciled to the change in the total GAAP net sales.
Reconciliation of GAAP measure "Working Capital" to Non-GAAP Measure "Trade Working Capital"
Due to the need to optimize cash generation to create value for shareholders, management focuses on operationally derived trade working capital as defined in the table below. Trade working capital is an indicator of operational efficiency, which impacts the Company’s ability to return value to shareholders either through dividends or share repurchases. We believe this is useful for readers to understand the efficiency of the Company’ operational capital management. The reconciling items used to derive this measure are, by contrast, managed as part of our overall management of cash and debt, but they are not part of the responsibilities of day-to-day operations management.

Net Debt
Autoliv from time to time enters into “debt-related derivatives” (DRDs) as a part of its debt management and as part of efficiently managing the Company’s overall cost of funds. Creditors and credit rating agencies use net debt adjusted for DRDs in their analyses of the Company’s debt, therefore, we provide this Non-GAAP measure. DRDs are fair value adjustments to the carrying value of the underlying debt. Also included in the DRDs is the unamortized fair value adjustment related to a discontinued fair value hedge that will be amortized over the remaining life of the debt. By adjusting for DRDs, the total financial liability of net debt is disclosed without grossing debt up with currency or interest fair values.

Items Affecting Comparability
We believe that comparability between periods is improved through the exclusion of certain items. To assist investors in understanding the operating performance of Autoliv's business, it is useful to consider certain GAAP measures exclusive of these items.

 
The following tables reconcile Income before income taxes, Net income attributable to controlling interest, Capital employed, which are inputs utilized to calculate Return on Capital Employed (“ROCE”), adjusted ROCE and Return on Total Equity (“ROE”). The Company believes this presentation may be useful to investors and industry analysts who utilize these adjusted non-U.S. GAAP measures in their ROCE and ROE calculations to exclude certain items for comparison purposes across periods. Autoliv’s management uses the ROCE, adjusted ROCE and ROE measures for purposes of comparing its financial performance with the financial performance of other companies in the industry and providing useful information regarding the factors and trends affecting the Company’s business.
 
As used by the Company, ROCE is annualized operating income and income from equity method investments, relative to average capital employed. Adjusted ROCE is annualized operating income and income from equity method investments, relative to average capital employed as adjusted to exclude certain non-recurring items. See definitions of "annualized operating income" and "average capital employed" in footnote to the tables below. The Company believes ROCE and adjusted ROCE are useful indicators of long-term performance both absolute and relative to the Company's peers as it allows for a comparison of the profitability of the Company’s capital employed in its business relative to that of its peers.
 
ROE is the ratio of annualized income (loss) relative to average total equity for the periods presented. See definitions of "annualized income" and "average total equity" in footnote to the tables below. Adjusted ROE is annualized income (loss) relative to average total equity for the periods presented as adjusted to exclude certain non-recurring items. The Company’s management believes that ROE and Adjusted ROE are useful indicators of how well management creates value for its shareholders through its operating activities and its capital management.
 
With respect to the Andrews litigation settlement, the Company has treated this specific settlement as a non-recurring charge because of the unique nature of the lawsuit, including the facts and legal issues involved.
 
Accordingly, the tables below reconcile from GAAP to the equivalent Non-GAAP measures.
